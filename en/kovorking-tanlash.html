Choosing a coworking space in Tashkent: 7 mistakes that cost you time and money

Why does a cheap coworking space end up costing more? The monthly price is in the listing, the losses are not. Let's count them together.

When choosing a coworking space, most people ask one question: "How much per month?" It is the right question, but not enough. The real price shows up at the end of the month: hours on the road, hunting for a free desk, "that's not included" charges and broken focus.

Below are the 7 most common mistakes people make when choosing a coworking space. Each one comes with a simple calculation: plug in your own numbers.

Mistake 1: looking only at the monthly price

Two places can have the same monthly price but include different things. In one, the printer, locker or computer are included; in another, they cost extra. Every "extra" you find out about after signing the contract raises your monthly price.

What to do: before asking about the price, ask "What is not included?" Where there is no clear answer to that question, an unexpected charge may appear at the end of the month.

At Makon, membership costs $250 per month. It includes your own dedicated desk, computers, Wi-Fi, a free printer, a free personal locker and books for residents. Payment is by QR code (UzQR) only, from your banking app.

Mistake 2: not counting the commute

A coworking space may be cheap, but if it is on the edge of the city, you pay for it with something else: your time.

Do the maths: say the round trip takes 1 hour a day. If you work 6 days a week, that is about 26 working days a month. So 26 hours a month go on the road. That is more than three full working days.

At Makon: central Tashkent, 11 Lashkarbegi Street, Akay City residential complex, 2nd floor. A 2-minute walk from Hamid Olimjon metro station. There is parking for those who drive, with a restaurant and shops nearby.

Mistake 3: working every day from a "hot desk"

With hot desks, whoever arrives first gets the seat. That works if you come once a week. If you work every day, the same small routine repeats daily: find a free desk, unpack your things, pack them up again in the evening.

Do the maths: even if it takes just 10 minutes a day, over 26 working days that is more than 4 hours. Add to that getting used to a new spot every day and having to rebuild your focus.

At Makon: every resident gets their own dedicated desk. There are 15 desks in total, each assigned to one person. There is also a free personal locker for your things. You arrive in the morning, sit down and start working.

Mistake 4: tying all your work to one laptop

If your laptop is left at home or in for repair, for most people work stops that day. Carrying it around every day is tiring in itself.

At Makon: there are computers for residents, included in the membership price. You can come and work without a laptop.

Mistake 5: not asking how many people work in the room

When a big hall holds hundreds of people, there is a lot of noise, phone calls and constant coming and going. In work that needs concentration, that takes the most valuable thing in your day: your attention.

At Makon: 15 desks in total, spread over two floors. The same people work here every day, with no stream of strangers.

Mistake 6: not planning where to meet clients

For a freelancer or business owner, the first meeting decides a lot. A meeting in a noisy café also leaves an impression of your work.

At Makon: there is a meeting room with a large white table, and a kitchen and dining area on the second floor. The rooms are bright, with black MAKON lettering on the walls. It is a place you can confidently invite a client to.

Mistake 7: renting just a desk and ignoring the people around you

In a coworking space you choose not just a desk but the people around you. The person at the next desk could, a year from now, be your client, your partner or the source of a new idea. If the people around you change every day, such connections hardly ever happen.

At Makon: people who have their own work and want to grow: students, targeting specialists, freelancers, entrepreneurs and travel agents. There are few desks, so people get to know each other well.

Adding it up

Do the maths: add up two simple examples: 1 hour of commuting a day and 10 minutes a day looking for a desk. Over a month that is more than 30 hours. So almost four working days a month go not on work but on the road and small hassles. When you compare coworking prices, count these hours too.
